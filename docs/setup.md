# Set it up on your iPhone or iPad

Budget about an hour. These steps are written for **Notes on iOS 27** or **iPadOS 27**. The exact label on a button can shift slightly between versions; when in doubt, look for the meaning in the table, not the icon.

## 1. Turn on iCloud Notes

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap your name, then **iCloud**.
3. Turn on **Notes**. On newer iOS or iPadOS versions it sits under **Apps** or **Show All** inside iCloud.
4. Open **Notes**. In the folder list, confirm you are looking at the **iCloud** account, not an email account and not **On My iPhone**.

Folders, tags, and Smart Folders in this guide all depend on iCloud notes. If old notes live in an email account, move the ones you still want into iCloud before you build PARA on top of them. Moving is a one-time chore; mixing accounts is a permanent mess.

## 2. Leave the built-in Notes folder as Inbox

You do not create Inbox. Apple already did.

New notes from the compose button, Siri (“Make a note…”), the share sheet, and Quick Note normally land in the built-in **Notes** folder. That is what you want. During setup, glance at Settings → Apps → Notes if you want to confirm the default account is iCloud.

Do not convert **Notes** into a Smart Folder.

## 3. Create the four PARA folders

1. Open **Notes**.
2. If you are inside a folder, tap **Folders** (or the back chevron) until you see the folder list.
3. Tap the **New Folder** button. Choose the **iCloud** account if the app asks.
4. Name it `Projects`. Repeat for `Areas`, `Resources`, and `Archives`.

Suggested order in the list, top to bottom: **Projects**, **Areas**, **Resources**, **Archives**. You can drag folders to reorder them. The built-in **Notes** folder will stay where Apple puts it; treat that position as Inbox, not as a ranking.

Leave the folders empty except for the purpose notes in the next step. Do not pre-make a subfolder for every area of your life.

## 4. Pin a purpose note in each folder

Repeat this for each row.

1. Open the folder.
2. Tap the compose button.
3. Type the title as the first line, then a single sentence in the body.
4. Tap Done.
5. In the notes list, swipe right on the note (or touch and hold it) and tap **Pin**.

| Folder | Purpose note title | Body |
| --- | --- |
| Projects | Projects: things with a finish line | Short-term work you are committed to finish. One note per project unless it grows several pages. |
| Areas | Areas: standards with no end date | Ongoing parts of life or work. Health, money, a job role. No finish line. |
| Resources | Resources: useful later | Topics you might need. Not a current commitment. |
| Archives | Archives: inactive on purpose | Finished, paused, or obsolete. Out of sight, still searchable. |

These four pins are there so you do not invent a fifth meaning at 11 p.m.

## 5. Make capture easy

Do whichever of these you will actually use:

- Add a **Notes** widget to the Home Screen or Lock Screen (Lock Screen widgets are iPhone-only). A widget that starts a new note beats a widget that only shows old ones.
- Add Notes to **Control Centre**, and turn on **Quick Note** if your version offers it there. On iPhone you can also assign Notes or Quick Note to the Action button.
- In Safari or Mail, use the share button once, choose Notes, and confirm the new note landed in the **Notes** folder.
- Tell Siri “Make a note: test inbox” once, and check where it appeared. If it filed into a folder you did not expect, check the default account in Settings → Apps → Notes.

Capture should be possible without deciding on a folder. If a shortcut or share destination asks you to pick **Projects** every time, you will stop capturing. Point it at Inbox.

## 6. Optional: two overlay tags

Skip this if you want zero tags. You can add them later from [optional extras](advanced.md).

1. Open any purpose note.
2. On a new line, type `#work` then a space, and `#personal` then a space. The words should change colour when Notes accepts them as tags.
3. Delete those two lines. The tags now exist in the tag browser at the bottom of the folder list.
4. Do not put `#work` on the purpose notes themselves.

## 7. Do not make these Smart Folders

In the folder list, look at **Projects**, **Areas**, **Resources**, and **Archives**. They must remain ordinary folders.

If you ever see **Make into Smart Folder** on those four, leave it alone. That command empties the folder.

A Smart Folder that shows untagged notes can wait. It is an extra view of Inbox, described in [advanced](advanced.md), and it is optional.

## 8. First pass on the old library

You do not have to sort your whole history today.

1. Open the built-in **Notes** folder.
2. Delete obvious junk: empty notes, duplicate shopping lists, expired codes. Recently Deleted holds them for a while if you panic.
3. For anything you already know is an active project, move it into **Projects**: in the notes list, swipe left (or touch and hold), tap **Move**, choose **Projects**.
4. Leave the rest. The [weekly review](weekly-review.md) is how the pile shrinks.

If you have old folders with names like Work and Holidays, you can drag those folders into **Archives** as a block and forget them until search needs them. You do not have to refile every historical note into PARA on day one.

## You are done when

- iCloud Notes is on, and you are not building this in an email account.
- **Projects**, **Areas**, **Resources**, and **Archives** exist as ordinary folders, each with a pinned purpose note.
- New capture still lands in the built-in **Notes** folder.
- You have deleted or moved a little of the old mess, and stopped before the afternoon became a migration project.
